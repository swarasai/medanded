Premed Takeover
Ingrid is a pre-med student from Miami who goes to FIU. View Ingrid answering audience questions, talking about time management, and taking you through a pre-med student’s daily life.

Some questions answered:

Ingrid introduced herself as a pre-med student from Miami, double majoring in biology and STEM education and minoring in chemistry. She walks viewers through a full day of classes, how she stays organized, and how she manages her time.

She also shares quick updates from biochemistry and from her teaching-focused science and math education classes.

As a Canadian high school student, would you recommend applying to colleges in the U.S.?

Ingrid explains that this is a personal decision. She recommends looking carefully at whether a U.S. school truly matches what you want from your undergraduate experience, especially if you plan to study and practice medicine in the U.S. She also points out that many schools, including FIU, have strong support for international students.

What class is your favorite this semester?

She talks about enjoying evolution and biochemistry most. Evolution is especially engaging because of the professor, class discussions, and topics such as fossils and paleontology. She also likes biochemistry because of her study group and her work as a chemistry learning assistant.

When did you switch to taking notes on a tablet / why do you prefer it?

Ingrid used notebooks and a touchscreen laptop through high school and freshman year, then moved to an iPad after seeing how useful it was for classmates. She likes being able to annotate slides, reorganize information, redraw chemistry structures, and turn messy class notes into clean study guides.

What sparked your interest in this major?

She describes growing up around science, loving both science and teaching, and choosing a combined STEM education path. She still plans to pursue medicine and sees teaching skills as valuable for residency, patient care, and potentially becoming a medical-school professor later.

Ingrid also describes her schedule as a chemistry learning assistant, including working with students in breakout rooms, holding office hours, reviewing material repeatedly, and building relationships with faculty that can lead to mentorship and research opportunities.

What extracurriculars did you do to get here?

She emphasizes that not every extracurricular needs to be pre-med related. Her activities included musical theatre, piano, dance, basketball, swimming, Model UN, honor society leadership, and pre-health organizations. She believes variety helped her become a more well-rounded applicant.

She also discusses leadership roles, teaching swim lessons, strong grades and ACT performance, and receiving a full-ride scholarship. Her broader advice is to pursue activities you actually enjoy and communicate clearly why those experiences matter to you.

What did you do in high school to be a competitive premed candidate?

Her advice is to focus on strong academics while also being well-rounded. She recommends sports, clubs, service, leadership, or other activities that genuinely interest you rather than doing things only to fill a résumé.

What college do you attend?

Ingrid attends Florida International University in Miami. She highlights its resources, professors, opportunities, and the fact that students do not need to attend a top-20 university to create a strong path toward medical school.

Are there any dream graduate programs for you? Do you want to stay in state?

She discusses interest in medical school programs including NYU Long Island and FIU’s medical school. She likes primary care and is weighing the advantages of staying near family in Florida against the experience of living somewhere new.

What would you suggest taking in grade 11 and 12?

She recommends taking science courses such as biology, chemistry, and physics where possible, along with math through calculus or statistics. She also stresses leaving time for extracurriculars, friends, sports, music, and activities that make school life balanced.

Any book recommendations?

She talks about enjoying reading outside school and mentions books and series she likes, while encouraging students to read widely because faster, stronger reading skills can also help later with the MCAT.

She closes the takeover by thanking Med n’ Ed for the opportunity and reminding viewers to take care of themselves, stay hydrated, and make room for rest even during busy school weeks.
